# A GUIDE TO SLOANE MAIRI
### Character Development Reference | Marigold

**Purpose:** To establish Sloane Mairi as an independent, fully realized character whose identity, motivations, values, and development exist beyond her relationship with Dain Aetos.

This guide distinguishes established characterization from interpretations and possible developmental directions.

---

## I. Core Character Identity

**Sloane Mairi is a naturally nurturing, fiercely independent young woman whose circumstances have forced her into a life fundamentally incompatible with the one she desires.**

She is rough around the edges, confrontational, stubborn, and fiercely protective of her dignity. Beneath that exterior, she is gentle, affectionate, generous, and deeply invested in the well-being of others.

Her disposition is not inherently hostile. Much of her hostility exists within the context of an environment she resents and relationships she struggles to navigate.

She doesn't want to fight. She doesn't want to follow in her brother's footsteps. She doesn't want to become a weapon for Basgiath.

She wants to be capable, useful, and free to determine what those qualities mean for herself.

**Central characterization principle:** Sloane does not need to become gentler, kinder, or more compassionate. She already possesses those qualities. Her development concerns the circumstances in which she feels comfortable expressing them.

## II. Personality and Temperament

### Outward disposition

- Bold, confrontational, and outspoken.
- Rougher around the edges than her brother, Liam.
- Strong-willed and unwilling to accept being underestimated.
- Resistant to authority when she feels her autonomy or competence is being undermined.
- Capable of considerable persistence and discipline.
- Guarded around people she doesn't fully trust.

### Comfortable disposition

When Sloane feels safe and relaxed, her behavior becomes noticeably softer.

She is naturally tactile in comfortable environments. She doesn't necessarily seek out physical affection, but incidental touch comes easily to her when she is at ease.

Her gentleness is not a separate personality that replaces her assertiveness. Both qualities coexist.

**Important distinction:** Attraction, affection, and comfort are not interchangeable. Sloane can be physically attracted to someone without trusting them or feeling relaxed in their presence.

## III. Personal Interests and Enjoyment

Sloane enjoys activities that allow her to contribute, create, and exercise practical skills.

**Established interests:**
- Volunteer work, particularly helping people who cannot help themselves.
- Cooking and baking.
- Needlework, including knitting and stitching.
- Tea.
- Sweet snacks and treats.

She finds fulfillment in doing things with her hands and developing abilities through practice.

Her enjoyment of volunteer work is especially meaningful. Sloane is not opposed to hard work or personal effort. She willingly invests herself in things she believes are worthwhile.

Her rejection of military life should never be confused with laziness, cowardice, or an unwillingness to contribute.

## IV. Personal Values

Sloane admires:

**Honesty:** She values sincerity and straightforwardness in others.

**Kindness:** She admires people who show consideration and compassion.

**Boundaries:** She especially respects people who protect their personal boundaries and refuse to tolerate unfair treatment.

**Fairness:** She strongly dislikes prejudice and the mistreatment of others.

**Capability:** She believes people can overcome considerable difficulties through the right attitude and sustained effort.

These qualities influence how she evaluates others, including Dain.

Her respect for kindness does not mean she expects people to tolerate mistreatment. In fact, she admires people who refuse to do so.

## V. Competence, Learning, and Persistence

### Sloane's approach to learning

**Sloane does not strive for perfection. She strives for the ability to execute.**

She practices something repeatedly until she can perform it confidently, reliably, and naturally.

Excellence is often the consequence of this approach rather than her conscious objective.

She doesn't necessarily seek to be the best. However, through persistent repetition and the natural development of proficiency, she often becomes exceptionally good at what she undertakes.

Her confidence rests in her belief that she can overcome difficulties through effort and the correct attitude.

### Relationship with failure

Failure does not inherently discourage Sloane.

When she encounters something she cannot do, her inclination is to practice until she can.

Her desire to improve is not necessarily motivated by external recognition, competition, or the pursuit of flawless results.

### Sensitivity to being underestimated

One of Sloane's strongest sensitivities is being perceived as incapable.

She dislikes being corrected publicly, particularly when the correction makes her appear incompetent.

She also dislikes being ignored or unacknowledged, although being underestimated is the more significant affront.

**Key distinction:** Sloane can reject the life of a rider without accepting the idea that she is incapable of becoming an excellent one.

Her personal pride and her loyalty to Basgiath are entirely separate matters.

## VI. Relationship with Basgiath

Sloane feels profoundly alienated within Basgiath and wants little to do with its military culture.

She does not want to fight or become a soldier. She does not aspire to follow Liam's path simply because he was her brother.

Her reluctance is not rooted in an inability to learn or improve.

Rather, she objects to the future Basgiath represents and the responsibilities it threatens to impose upon her.

### Her imagined future

Sloane imagines living quietly in a bustling city.

She wants a peaceful existence without fighting.

Importantly, her ideal life is not necessarily isolated. She enjoys helping people, creating things, and existing within a community.

She wants peace without needing to withdraw from the world.

## VII. Siphoning and Her Relationship with Power

Sloane's distaste for her signet stems primarily from the possibility of being made into a weapon for Basgiath.

Her objection is not fundamentally to possessing power or developing competence.

She does not want to hurt people.

The existence of an ability that can be used to harm others threatens to place her in a role she finds morally objectionable.

### Her relationship with Dain's signet

Sloane does **not** wish to possess Dain's signet.

What matters to her is that Dain cannot directly injure someone through his own signet.

Dain, meanwhile, wishes he possessed a more formidable offensive capability.

Their conflict is therefore not a simple exchange of envy between two wielders.

**Sloane does not want to harm people. Dain wishes he had the power to confront threats more directly.**

Dain recognizes that there is strength in being something other than a weapon, even though he struggles with his own desire for greater offensive power.

### Potential developmental direction

Learning to wield does not have to mean Sloane accepts becoming a weapon.

Her development may involve distinguishing between possessing an ability and consenting to the purposes for which others want to use it.

This is a possible thematic direction, not an established resolution to her conflict.

## VIII. Liam Mairi: Her Brother

### Before their parents' deaths

Sloane and Liam were extremely close.

Liam was infinitely gentle, respectful, and patient, particularly with his younger sister.

He consistently made time for her and encouraged her to pursue her own interests.

Sloane admired those qualities because they differed from aspects of her own temperament.

She was rougher around the edges, but she loved Liam's gentleness.

**Sloane admired Liam without wanting to become him.**

He respected who she was rather than expecting her to resemble him.

### After their parents' deaths

Following the rebellion, the marked children were separated and fostered into different households, largely among noble families.

Liam and Sloane maintained contact primarily through letters.

Their physical separation did not mean they willingly abandoned their relationship.

### Liam's first year at Basgiath

When Liam entered Basgiath, their correspondence was interrupted because first-year cadets could neither send nor receive letters.

Sloane therefore had limited direct knowledge of his experiences and the relationships he developed during that time.

### After Liam's death

Sloane learns about Liam's experiences at Basgiath through letters Violet possesses.

Violet uses access to those letters to incentivize Sloane to train, because Sloane initially has no desire to participate.

This means her initial motivation for training is connected to the opportunity to learn more about her brother, rather than enthusiasm for military life.

### Liam's lasting significance

Liam represents an established experience of patience, kindness, and respect.

He encouraged Sloane to become herself instead of following his example.

His death does not create Sloane's compassionate qualities. Those qualities were already part of her identity.

**Characterization principle:** Liam's significance must be preserved without reducing Sloane to her grief.

## IX. Relationships Beyond Liam

### Violet Sorrengail

Violet possesses Liam's letters and allows Sloane access to them as an incentive for training.

Through these letters, Sloane begins learning about her brother's experiences and relationships at Basgiath.

The arrangement creates a connection between Sloane's grief, her reluctant participation in training, and Violet's involvement in her life.

Sloane's fuller emotional interpretation of this arrangement remains open for development.

### Imogen Cardulo

Sloane remains reluctant to open up to Imogen despite their existing connection and Imogen's involvement in training.

Her guardedness should not be mistaken for a fundamental dislike of companionship.

The reasons she struggles to become emotionally available to Imogen remain an area for further exploration.

### Xaden Riorson

Sloane does not personally know Xaden.

His significance to Liam's life does not automatically translate into a personal relationship between Xaden and Sloane.

Her understanding of him should reflect what she has actually learned rather than knowledge she has never possessed.

## X. Dain Aetos: Attraction, Resentment, and Familiarity

Sloane experiences intense attraction toward Dain while simultaneously carrying conflicting feelings regarding his involvement in Liam's death.

Her attraction does not eliminate her resentment, and her resentment does not prevent her from recognizing qualities she genuinely admires.

### Qualities she may appreciate in Dain

- Gentleness.
- Patience.
- Intelligence and competence.
- Attentiveness.
- Respect for her potential.
- Willingness to dedicate time to her development.

Dain possesses several qualities that Sloane already values in other people.

### The resemblance between Liam and Dain

Both men are notably gentle and patient.

Sloane recognizes these qualities in Dain, but does not immediately connect them to the qualities she admired in Liam.

**The reader should recognize the resemblance before Sloane consciously puts the pieces together.**

This resemblance does not mean Dain replaces Liam or that Sloane's attraction is rooted in seeking a substitute for her brother.

Rather, it reflects continuity in the qualities Sloane has always valued.

### An important difference

Liam's patience and kindness toward Sloane arose within an established, loving sibling relationship.

Dain's treatment of her is complicated by guilt, remorse, responsibility, and his determination to make restitution for Liam's death.

The reader should be able to distinguish Dain's genuine temperament from the obligations he places upon himself.

Sloane may need time to distinguish those things herself.

### Dain's self-sacrificial behavior

Sloane admires people who protect their boundaries and reject unfair treatment.

Dain frequently tolerates her hostility and expresses a willingness to sacrifice himself on her behalf.

This creates a possible point of conflict.

She may understand his guilt without believing that he should continue accepting punishment or treating his own life as expendable.

Her precise interpretation of this behavior remains open for development.

## XI. Dain and Sloane's Contrasting Approaches to Competence

**Dain tends to do things correctly the first time. Sloane develops exceptional competence through repetition.**

Dain's aptitude allows him to understand and execute things quickly.

Sloane's persistence allows her to make abilities reliable and natural through continued practice.

These different approaches do not inherently make either character superior.

They provide opportunities for mutual respect, misunderstanding, and growth.

As Sloane's instructor, Dain must distinguish between correcting her technique and inadvertently making her feel underestimated.

As his trainee, Sloane can learn from his aptitude without surrendering her own approach to developing competence.

## XII. Their Relationship's Developmental Foundation

### Attraction is already present

Sloane is extremely attracted to Dain.

The degree to which she consciously acknowledges or accepts that attraction remains to be clarified.

### Trust is still developing

Physical attraction does not imply emotional comfort.

Sloane can want Dain while remaining guarded, resentful, or uncertain about him.

### Familiarity needs development

Dain knows Sloane largely through her grief, her abilities, her hostility, and his responsibility toward her.

Sloane knows Dain largely through his role as her instructor, his history with Liam, and his efforts to help her.

Their relationship needs opportunities for each to discover the other's ordinary personality.

### Ordinary interactions

Not every encounter should revolve around Liam, training, siphoning, guilt, or forgiveness.

Ordinary interactions can establish:
- Shared humor and conversational compatibility.
- Each character's habits and preferences.
- The distinction between attraction and genuine enjoyment of one another's company.
- Small changes in trust and comfort.
- The ability to interact without having to resolve their complicated history.

**Developmental principle:** Sloane and Dain should gradually discover whether they actually enjoy each other as people, independently of their obligations and attraction.

## XIII. Character Contradictions Worth Preserving

**She is compassionate but confrontational.** Her kindness does not require politeness or passivity.

**She rejects military life but refuses to be incompetent.** Her pride in her abilities does not constitute loyalty to Basgiath.

**She admires gentleness but is rougher herself.** She doesn't have to imitate the qualities she admires.

**She is capable of great affection but remains emotionally guarded.** Her comfortable behavior differs from her defensive behavior.

**She can master an ability she doesn't want to use.** Competence does not automatically create enthusiasm.

**She resents Dain while admiring aspects of his character.** Those feelings can coexist without immediate reconciliation.

**She wants a peaceful life but possesses a potentially dangerous signet.** Her abilities and aspirations are not naturally aligned.

## XIV. Questions Still Open for Development

These aspects of Sloane have not yet been fully established:

1. How consciously does she recognize her attraction to Dain?
2. How does she feel about Violet using Liam's letters to motivate her training?
3. What prevents her from becoming emotionally comfortable with Imogen?
4. Does she recognize Dain's willingness to endure mistreatment as a problem?
5. How has her experience of foster care affected her relationships and sense of belonging?
6. How does she respond when she begins to feel comfortable around Dain outside training?
7. What does she want from Dain personally, beyond instruction, attraction, and answers about the past?
8. How does she reconcile developing a formidable ability with her refusal to harm others?

These should remain unanswered until supported by Sloane's established characterization or deliberate future decisions.

---

## XV. Core Characterization Reminder

Sloane Mairi is not simply Liam's grieving sister, Dain's reluctant trainee, or a potential romantic partner.

She is a young woman who loves helping people, creating things, developing practical skills, and experiencing the quieter pleasures of life.

She admires kindness without demanding softness, respects competence without worshipping perfection, and believes persistent effort can overcome seemingly insurmountable obstacles.

She wants a life in which she can contribute to the world without being required to hurt it.

**She does not need to become more like Liam, more like Dain, or more like the rider Basgiath expects her to be.**

Her development should allow her to become more fully herself.
